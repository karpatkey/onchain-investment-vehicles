Where the watermark lands

A kpk fund with a performance fee charges it only on gains above a reference price. The first three modules in src/FeeModules/ are plain high watermarks that differ on one detail: where the watermark sits after a fee has been paid, which decides how much of the configured rate is actually collected. The other six change what counts as a gain, or when it is charged.

What every module does the same

Every module shares WatermarkFeeBase. It keeps one reference price per fund (the highWatermark mapping), keyed by the calling KpkShares proxy, so one deployment can serve many funds and a stranger calling the module directly only moves their own entry.

The first call seeds the reference at the price it is given and charges nothing. After that each module decides whether the price owes a fee, how many fee shares to mint, and where the reference goes next; a call that changes nothing leaves no trace. For the three watermark modules, a price at or below the watermark charges nothing and leaves the watermark where it is. The timeElapsed argument is passed through to an event; the hurdle and period modules keep their own clock.

Nothing here moves money directly. The module returns a number of shares, and KpkShares mints them to the fee receiver. Minting dilutes every holder, which is how the fee is paid, and it is why the share price drops after a charge.

Three size a plain high-watermark fee in different ways. The other six change what counts as a gain, or how often it is charged. None is deployed yet.

One fund, three modules

Alice holds all 1,000 investor shares at $1.00, so NAV is $1,000. The performance fee is 20%, the fee receiver starts with no shares, and the operator always reports price as NAV divided by total supply. The first settlement seeds every module's watermark at $1.00.

Under WatermarkFee the price climbs from $1.1613 back to exactly the $1.20 watermark, so nothing is above it and no fee is charged on a real $38.71 gain. That fee-free band, the gap between the price after a fee and the watermark left above it, reopens after every charge. The other two modules have no band and collect on the whole rise.

When the fee receiver already holds shares

Minting dilutes every share, including the ones the fee receiver already owns. Part of each fee is therefore paid by the receiver to itself. That happens whenever the receiver keeps earlier fee shares instead of redeeming them, so it grows with every charge.

Same fund, but the receiver also holds 500 shares: NAV $1,500 rises to $1,800, price $1.00 to $1.20. Alice's gain is still $200 and her fair fee still $40.

Over many charges

The two effects compound: the fee-free band reopens after every WatermarkFee charge, and self-dilution grows as the receiver accumulates shares. Simulating a fund that keeps rising, at a configured 20%, gives the rate each module actually collects.

The formulas

With price P, watermark W, rate r, investor shares N (netSupply) and total supply S. Each block is the fee step of the module's _crystallize; when P ≤ W all three return first, charging nothing and leaving the watermark where it is.

WatermarkFee

fee = (P − W) · N / P · r; watermark → P

uint256 totalProfit = ((sharesPrice - watermark) * netSupply) / sharesPrice;
fee = (totalProfit * feePct) / 10_000;
newWatermark = sharesPrice;

PostFeeWatermarkFee

fee as above; watermark → P · S / (S + fee), the price after the mint

(fee, newWatermark) = super._crystallize(sharesPrice, watermark, feePct, netSupply);
if (fee == 0) return (fee, newWatermark);
uint256 supply = IERC20(msg.sender).totalSupply();
newWatermark = Math.mulDiv(sharesPrice, supply, supply + fee, Math.Rounding.Ceil);

ExactWatermarkFee

per-share fee a = r · (P − W); fee = a · S / (P − a); watermark → P − a

// scaledFee = (sharesPrice - watermark) * feePct, i.e. a * 10_000
if (netSupply == 0) return (0, sharesPrice);
uint256 supply = IERC20(msg.sender).totalSupply();
fee = Math.mulDiv(scaledFee, supply, sharesPrice * 10_000 - scaledFee);
newWatermark = fee == 0 ? sharesPrice : sharesPrice - scaledFee / 10_000;

Minting a · S / (P − a) shares leaves every share worth P − a. Investors lose exactly a per share. The receiver's existing shares lose a too, but its new shares are worth a · S, so it nets a · N: the fee on investor shares and nothing on its own.

This sizing lives in _sizeExact, and every module in the next section reuses it with its own a. Every module except WatermarkFee reads totalSupply() from the calling fund and assumes the reported price is NAV ÷ total supply at that moment. KpkShares mints the management fee just before calling, so a price struck before that mint leaves the watermark slightly above the true post-fee price, by about the management fee's share of supply, in investors' favour.

Other ways to decide what counts as a gain

The three modules above charge any gain above the watermark at every settlement. The standard alternatives change one of two things: what the gain is measured against, or how often it is charged. All six size their fee exactly like ExactWatermarkFee, so every difference below comes from the rule, not from dilution arithmetic. Each takes a crystallizationPeriod in its constructor; 0 means every settlement.

Hurdles

A hurdle is a minimum return investors keep before a fee is due. Here it is the watermark grown by hurdleBps a year, as simple interest from the moment the watermark last moved: at 10% a year, a year after the watermark was set at $1.00 the hurdle is $1.10, and two years under water it is $1.20. The three hurdle modules differ in what they charge once it is cleared.

ModuleFee per shareInvestors keepShare of the gain charged

HardHurdleFee charges 20% of the gain above the hurdle only: at $1.20 that is 20% of $0.10, $0.02 a share. Investors keep the hurdle return whatever the fund earns.

SoftHurdleFee charges nothing up to $1.10 and then 20% of the whole gain from $1.00. The fee jumps at the hurdle: at $1.0975 investors keep the whole $0.0975, at $1.1025 they keep $0.082 after a $0.0205 fee.

CatchUpHurdleFee removes the jump. Above the hurdle it takes catchUpBps of each further gain, here all of it, until the fee reaches 20% of the whole gain at $1.125, and charges the full rate from there. At $1.12 it charges $0.02, where soft charges $0.024 and hard $0.004.

With no period, a fund that keeps rising is charged at every settlement, so each hurdle is measured over the few hours since the previous charge: 10% a year over 6 hours is about 0.007%. The hard hurdle still leaves investors the hurdle return over time, but the soft and catch-up hurdles are cleared by almost any gain over so short a window, so they only bite with a crystallization period such as a year.

Crystallization period

PeriodicWatermarkFee is ExactWatermarkFee that charges at most once per period: a settlement before the period has run since the last charge changes nothing, and the first one after it charges on whatever gain is above the watermark at that moment. ESMA's guidelines make once a year the default for European funds, with an exception for a whole-life watermark like ours.

Nothing accrues between charges, because the operator's price carries no pending performance fee. An investor who redeems mid-period pays none of that period's fee; one who subscribes mid-period pays it on gains made before they joined. The longer the period, the larger both effects. Traditional funds avoid this by accruing the fee inside the published NAV, which would need kpkShares changes.

Benchmark-relative

BenchmarkFee charges only for beating a benchmark read from a Chainlink-style feed, for example ETH/USD or a staking-rate index. The watermark is carried forward at the benchmark's return: set at $1.00 when the benchmark stood at 2,000, it becomes $1.10 when the benchmark reaches 2,200, and the fee is 20% of the price above that.

A stale, non-positive or reverting feed makes the call a no-op: nothing is seeded or charged, and the watermark and its benchmark level stay put, so the fee is deferred rather than lost and settlements keep working. The feed address is a constructor argument, so it is part of the module's address: a fund whose shares live on several chains cannot use one BenchmarkFee address everywhere if the feed address differs per chain.

No watermark

NoWatermarkFee drops the loss carry-forward: each charge is 20% of the gain since the previous reference, and a fall moves the reference down. Investors then pay again for recovering their own losses. It is here for comparison, not as a candidate.

What no module fixes

These come from how KpkShares calls the module, so they apply to every module. Each was reproduced in a Foundry test against WatermarkFee on 2026-11-04; the figures below are from those runs unless a module is named. Fixing them means changing the audited kpkShares.sol.

Redemptions in the charging settlement skip the fee

Requests are approved at the price the operator passed in, which is the price before the fee shares were minted. Alice and Bob each hold 1,000 shares, NAV goes from $2,000 to $2,400, and Bob redeems in the settlement that charges the fee. Bob is paid $1,200 and pays nothing. Alice, whose fair fee is $40, ends up paying $75.00. Under Exact the same arithmetic gives $77.42.

Turning the fee off and on charges the gap

While the rate is 0 or no module is set, KpkShares does not call the module, so the watermark stays put. Seed at $1.00, set the rate to 0, let the price reach $1.20, set it back to 20%: the next settlement at an unchanged $1.20 mints 33.33 shares for gains made while the advertised fee was zero.

One watermark for every fee asset

Each asset flagged isFeeModuleAsset is priced in its own unit but feeds the same watermark. With USDT at $0.995, a USDT settlement at a flat USD NAV reads as a 0.5% gain and is charged. The first settlement in a newly added asset also skips the 30% price-deviation check: a mistyped price of 2.00 minted 100 shares, 9.09% of supply, in one call.

Choosing a module

ExactWatermarkFee
Use when the fee collected has to match the configured rate, whatever the receiver does with its fee shares.
PostFeeWatermarkFee
Within 0.2 points of the configured rate when the receiver redeems its fee shares at each charge. Drifts down to 15.8–18.2% at 20% while it holds them.
WatermarkFee
The original module. Collects about 16.7% of a configured 20% even in its best case, less when the receiver holds shares. Choose it only if that discount is intended.
HardHurdleFee
Use when investors should keep a minimum return fee-free. Works at any crystallization period.
SoftHurdleFee, CatchUpHurdleFee
Use with a crystallization period, typically a year; at every settlement almost any gain clears the hurdle. Prefer the catch-up, which avoids the jump in the fee at the hurdle.
PeriodicWatermarkFee
Use when fees should crystallize on a calendar, as ESMA's once-a-year default expects. Investors who redeem mid-period escape that period's fee; those who subscribe mid-period pay for gains made before they joined.
BenchmarkFee
Use when the mandate is to beat a market. Set requireAbsoluteGain unless charging in a year when investors lost money is intended. Needs a feed at the same address on every chain the shares live on.
NoWatermarkFee
For comparison only: investors pay again for recovering their own losses.
Every module
None is deployed yet. The module address is part of a fund's shares salt, so a fund whose shares live on several chains needs the module at the same address on each. Constructor arguments are part of that address, so each hurdle, period or feed setting is a separate deployment, and there is no deterministic deploy script for these modules yet.
